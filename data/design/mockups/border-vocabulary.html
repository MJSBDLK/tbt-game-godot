Border Vocabulary

One rule: a lit border means you can press it. Above that, three kinds of motion carry three meanings — none (idle), converging (come here), orbiting (you are here). Motions are stepped, not smooth, so they tick like pixel animation. Colors are game palette; 1 game px = 3 screen px. Font is a stand-in — judge the borders, not the glyphs.

The six states

Every card is live. Hover (or tab to) anything lit; click to feel the press response — one game pixel down, brightness flash, and the tick. Press the disabled one too.

Static unlit · never moves

Terrain Infopanels, labels, readouts

MEANING — this is furniture. You cannot press it.

Flat dark border, no light. Text keeps its GlowLabel halo — that's typography, not affordance.

Interactive · idle lit · steady

MEANING — you can press this. If it ain't lit, you can't click.

Steady, never pulsing. Ten of these on screen stay calm; the light alone carries the contract.

Focus / hover backlight · 8-frame fade

MEANING — the cursor (or controller focus) is on this one right now.

Same button twice: the bottom one cycles into its hovered state — the border brightens and the button's background lifts, like a backlight switching on behind it. The lift fades in over 2/15 s (~8 frames) in 4 discrete shades, not a smooth glide (the old shine sweep read too hifi; ¼ s felt too slow). On phones this state barely exists; the press-down response does the work instead.

Disabled darkest · steady

MEANING — exists, but not now. Press it to learn why.

Darker than static so it never reads as lit. Tapping surfaces the reason — matters most on touch, where there's no hover to explain anything.

Selected corner ticks · the pick

MEANING — you are here. Only one thing can be selected, so only one exists.

Hot-white corner ticks that snap between exactly two positions — in (hugging the border) and one game px out — no easing between, at 1.25 Hz (tune with the “Bracket snap” slider). The button itself keeps its idle colors: purple was retired from selection (kill your darlings, 2026-07-15) so the shape is the whole signal. 2 px overhang at peak, zero layout cost.

Selected · orbit alternate · marquee

MEANING — same state, kept as the runner-up.

Two highlights on opposite sides of the border, traveling around it like a marquee at a fixed speed — near-white core, shades stepping down to the (now azure) border color on each side, so the tails still melt into the frame. Constant size and speed no matter the button shape. Tune it with the Marquee slider.

Call to action converging rings

MEANING — the game suggests this next. At most ONE on screen, ever.

Rings appear dim a few px out and shrink onto the border, which catches the light as they land. Motion toward the button points at it — nothing else in the set moves across the screen like this, so it can't be confused with selected or hovered.

⚠ The plain pulse rejected · shown for comparison

WHY WE'RE NOT USING IT — pulsing in place looks like every other glowing thing.

Side by side with the converging rings, this just reads as a brighter normal button — and it's easy to mistake for a selected one. Kept here so the comparison is one glance, not an argument.

Move chips

The vocabulary over themed bodies: a chip's element colors, usage fill, and its own gray rounded border are all skin (exactly the in-engine chip shader), and no interactivity state may repaint them except the disabled grey-down. The vocabulary contributes only the brackets, a one-step luminance lift, press, and deny. Everything here is live.

Usable lit · steady

MEANING — pressable, but the border belongs to the SKIN, not the vocabulary.

The chip keeps its own themed border (gray, rounded, exactly the in-engine chip shader) and a skewed usage boundary — 3 of 5 here. On chips the lit/unlit contract reads from the vivid body vs the grey-down; the vocabulary contributes backlight, brackets, press, and deny.

Hover it: the whole body lifts one step up its own palette ramp (both fill and empty together, so the usage boundary keeps its contrast and can't misread as a uses change). The mockup fakes the ramp step with a white lift; in-engine it's Orange 6 → Orange 7.

Cursor vs assigned snapping vs parked

MEANING — top: the cursor is here (snapping). Bottom: this move is ASSIGNED (same brackets, parked).

Same shape, same white — motion category carries the difference, the vocabulary's core rule (RQD test: no recolor; that's the fallback if this muddles). It replaces the old “> ” text prefix. Cursor landing on the assigned chip snaps its brackets — correct, that IS “you are here”.

Depleted darkest · press for why

Snugness rig

The real worry: the action menu is a 140-game-px column with 2 px gaps — and the real menu is chips on top, text buttons below. Here's every state coexisting at exactly that spacing: Ember holds the cursor, Frost Lance is the assigned move (parked brackets), Spark is depleted. Click around; selection moves with you.

— Actions —

What to eyeball here:

  • Do the converge rings read in a 2 px gap? They spawn 6 game px out — at this spacing they briefly cross the neighbor. If that's noise, first knob to turn is spawn distance (6 → 3 px), not the effect.
  • Brackets in tight quarters — the pick overhangs up to 2 game px at peak; check whether it crowds the neighbor at 2 px gaps, and tune the snap rate live. The Selected style control still offers the marquee orbit (runner-up) for comparison.
  • Busyness ceiling: at most two things animate at once (one selection + one call to action). Everything else is steady by rule, so the panel stays calm even fully populated.
  • Attention order should read: disabled < static < normal buttons < selected < call to action. Squint test: End Turn should win your eye, Attack second. With no purple recolor, the brackets carry selection alone — squint and confirm Attack still reads second.
  • Cursor vs assigned on chips — Frost Lance keeps parked brackets while the cursor snaps elsewhere; move the cursor ONTO it and the brackets snap (that IS “you are here”). If parked-vs-snapping muddles at a glance, the fallback is recoloring the assigned set — but test the pure version first.
  • Reduce motion caveat: with motion off, parked (assigned) and selected brackets become identical — the one known degradation of the no-recolor test. If Lawrence flags it, assigned gets a static distinguisher (recolor or half-arms) under reduce-motion only.
  • Reduce motion (top strip) collapses everything to static tiers — the call to action and selection keep their bright color, lose their motion. With brackets that means parked white ticks. This is the whole accessibility implementation, one flag.

Chip data — decided direction

Locked at the Lawrence review (2026-07-19): target schemes stay on the chip (worth the ink for new players), range is on-chip but never beside uses (two number pairs side by side confuse), the damage type icon tries the left slot beside the element (“if it's ugly we'll move it back”), and the full spec sheet moves to the long-press tooltip. The eyeball call landed: scheme + digits (RQD 2026-07-19); the strip stays as the runner-up record. Specimens as before — Spark (phys, melee), F. Lance (phys, 1–2), Fireball (spec, 2–3 + blast), First Aid (support, ally, no element).

Reach strip — runner-up, kept for reference
Scheme + digits — CHOSEN (RQD 2026-07-19)

The decisions, recorded:

  • Damage type sits beside the element — a trial with a revert clause. The real sprites already exist (move_type_icons_10x10, shipped in the detail panel, equipment picker, and combat preview), and special_d is already a magenta sparkle — the retired darling's new job turns out to be SPECIAL damage. The F6 gallery shows the real art; these glyphs approximate it.
  • Range never touches uses. “1-2” next to “2/3” is two number pairs asking to be misread. The range column sits mid-chip — a fixed-width name column keeps it aligned down the menu — and uses keeps the right edge to itself.
  • The tooltip is the new home of the full spec sheet. CORE input decision: long press (touch) = right click (mouse) = Back/R3 (controller) opens the move tooltip — the detail panel's content, with explanations. Long-press duration is a Settings slider, 200–1000 ms in 50 ms steps, default 200 ms. The floor is a softlock guard: below a hold the player can reliably execute, ordinary taps start reading as long-presses and pressing becomes impossible.
  • Strip vs digits — RESOLVED: scheme + digits (RQD 2026-07-19). Digits read instantly; the strip's learning cost lost. In-engine now: a runtime-drawn 10×10 glyph (TargetSchemeGlyph — crosshair = single, plus-cluster = blast, bone = hostile / teal = friendly) plus a “1-N” band in its own column, fixed name width keeping the columns aligned. The board remains the truth: focusing a chip should live-paint real range + footprint on the grid.
  • Glyph growth is sanctioned (RQD 2026-07-19). Little X marks inside the footprint may designate the damaged cells — which also notates donuts (footprint minus epicenter). An X wants a ≥3×3-px cell (at 2×2, degrade to center dots), so X-marked diameter 3 ≈ 11 px and diameter 5 ≈ 19 px: when fidelity needs it, the glyph outgrows 10×10 and the chip expands to fit — wider first, then the two-line layout. The grid preview stays the truth for anything the glyph abbreviates.
  • Numbers match the name's font scheme. Uses and range now inherit the chip text's color + glow instead of their old flat semi-white (RQD leaning, 2026-07-19). The artifact renders too hi-res to judge a 1-px halo — the REAL eyeball venue is the F6 gallery, where both labels now wear the name's GlowLabel treatment at true integer scale. Depleted numbers inherit the tier's grey with everything else.
  • Column caveat: First Aid has no element icon, so its row starts a slot early — the engine chip does the same today. If the ragged left edge grates, reserve the element slot even when empty.

Density crisis — power joins, venues diverge

First F5 pass (RQD 2026-07-19): the one-line chip is cramped at 120 px, overflows the detail panel's ~90 px column, and now power wants on-chip too — "THERE'S NO ROOM." The proposal: stop making one chip carry everything. Field sets per venue — full data where the battle decision happens, identity where a pane already shows the numbers, and a distinct non-interactive look where nothing is pressable.

Two-line, full data (action menu)
Identity only (detail selectors)
Display mode (preview) — 3 looks

The questions on the table:

  • Two-line is the structural answer to "no room". Line 1 = identity (element, damage type, the FULL name — no more 38 px clip column), line 2 = data (scheme, range … uses). Power rides line 1's right edge in the damage type's color (bone claw / magenta burst / teal cross), which keeps it from misreading as range or uses — the two-number-pair trap, again. Support moves show no power at all: mark the unusual. Cost: the action menu's four chips go from 4 to ~8 lines tall. Vertical expansion, only where data-complete matters.
  • Identity-only chips for the detail panel. The detail pane ALREADY shows Power/Rng/Acc/Usg in its readout — chips there are selectors, not spec sheets. Element + damage type + full name, nothing else. This dissolves the overflow bug's cause instead of clipping it.
  • Display mode needs its own look (the Bonk lesson: a vivid chip with brackets reads as pressable/selected). Three candidates on the same chip: borderless (the border is furniture's pressable cue — a data row doesn't get one), one ramp step down (element-true but unpowered; engine does a real ramp step, this mocks it with a filter), compact borderless (shorter, reads as a table row). Whatever wins must NOT read as depleted — all three stay on the element's colors, and none backlights on hover.
  • Assigned marker outside the action menu — open. Parked brackets are now an action-menu-only signal (they read as "selected" in static venues). If display venues need an assigned marker at all, it needs a non-bracket shape — candidates welcome, default is to not show it.
  • Venue width rule of thumb: never grow a chip horizontally past its venue's column; grow vertically only to become data-complete. Everything else is a field-set decision, not a layout decision.

Assigned ≠ selected — the marker hunt

RQD 2026-07-19: parked-vs-snapping brackets conflate two concepts — assignment is a persistent fact about the unit, selection is where the cursor is. If assignment gets its own STATIC marker, brackets go back to meaning exactly one thing ("you are here"), it works in display venues, and the reduce-motion degradation (parked ≈ snapping) dissolves for free. Three candidates on Frost Lance; Ember holds the cursor for contrast. Bone-white on purpose — azure is interactivity, amber is the CTA monopoly.

Cursor (for contrast) + 3 assigned markers

The candidates:

  • Edge bar — a 2px bone tab flush against the left edge, inside the border radius. Reads as a bookmark; survives every venue including display mode; can't be mistaken for the cursor because it never moves and has no corners.
  • Underline — a 1px bone bar along the chip's bottom inset. Quietest of the three; risk: sits near the usage fill's bottom edge and may tangle with it on low-fill chips.
  • Pip — a static ▸ before the element icon. Yes, this is the old "> " reborn as a glyph instead of text — the objection was the TEXT prefix mangling the name column; a fixed-width pip column may read fine. Cheapest to build.
  • The payoff if any of these wins: parked brackets RETIRE. Brackets mean only "you are here," the assigned marker works identically in the action menu, preview, and detail panel, and the known reduce-motion caveat (parked and snapping collapse into the same look) simply stops existing.
  • Depleted + assigned composes: the marker rides the dark tier unchanged (it's a fact, not an affordance) — whichever candidate wins should be checked against the grey body before locking.

Engine notes

One global clock

Every animation here is a multiple of a single --clock. In-engine: one autoload tween or a shared shader time uniform, so the UI breathes as one instead of shimmering out of phase. Also makes “reduce motion” a single kill switch — Settings.ui_motion_enabled, same pattern as portrait effects. When it's off, the call to action and the selection keep their bright colors but stop moving.

Stepped, not smooth

All motion uses stepped timing (the CSS is steps(n); in Godot, quantize the tween's interpolated value to frames). Smooth glides read as “modern app” — ticking reads as pixel art and stays crisp on the integer grid.

Integer-pixel press

Press response is translateY(1 game px) + a brightness flash — no scaling, ever, so nothing leaves the pixel grid. Pairs with the tap-feedback ring already shipped at the input layer.

Sound sketch

The ticks here are synthesized square-wave blips — hover ~2.4 kHz / 25 ms, press ~900 Hz with a noise transient, deny = low double-knock. A gesture toward RE1 / Deus Ex crispy; real samples are Lawrence's call, but the shape (sharp attack, dead-fast decay, mid-band) is the spec.
