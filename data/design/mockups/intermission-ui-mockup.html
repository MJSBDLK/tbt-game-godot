Hub states

The sub-lines are live — deploy or bench someone in 2b, spend a StatUp, buy a level, and these follow. Click Save Game to watch it settle; touch anything in 2b and it arms again.

Why a hub at all
  • Your call: a list with Manage Units on top. The hub is what makes room for the things that aren't unit editing — the briefing, the deploy, the system tail — without stapling them onto the workspace's top bar.
  • Venue split (new rule). The hub is the menus venue: bare glowing text, corner ticks, one lit-border default. The workspaces are the HUD venue: glass panels, full button vocabulary. Bare text needs a quiet ground; dense data needs a plate. The backdrop dims harder under a workspace.
  • Unfinished business is advertised, never enforced. The StatUp count rides Manage Units; the banked amount rides the bEXP row. Begin Mission is never blocked or confirmed — that's the squad manager's no-confirmations rule, and "are you sure? you have points left" is exactly the nag this project doesn't ship.
  • Save Game latches (round 11). After saving it reads Game saved! and goes unlit until you change something — the success voice says it worked, the unlit border says there's nothing to press. Two locked vocabularies composing instead of a toast. Consequence: if the campaign already autosaves at mission boundaries, this entry arrives pre-latched and mostly sits there reading "Game saved!" — which is either reassuring or an argument for deleting it.
  • "Allocate Bonus EXP" is a deep link, not a second screen. It opens Manage Units with the level row pre-selected and the roster sorted level-ascending — so the units the catch-up pricing exists for are already on top. That makes it a different task view instead of a duplicate door. (Try it.)
  • Mission Briefing keeps the objectives, drops the par terms (round 11). No "par 6 / dawdle 12" schedule on the hub. Unresolved: the bEXP amendment says par bands are a displayed fact — the FERD sin was making players learn the turn bonus from a wiki — and the briefing was the place that debt got paid. Two ways out: (a) speed shows up as a bonus objective in the objective list ("Clear within 6 turns"), so it's disclosed without ever reading as a par table, or (b) it's disclosed only after the fact on the result screen, which already itemizes income. (a) keeps the doctrine, (b) revises it. Your call.
  • Open: does the hub survive at all, or should arriving drop you straight into Manage Units with Begin Mission in its top bar? Cheap to test either way.

You were right not to trust the mockup — so it's measured now
  • The three engine fonts are embedded and rendering: UndeadPixelLight8 @8px (Godot's theme default), UndeadPixelLight11 @11px for names, NotJamPixel5 @5px for the tiny caps labels. Letter-spacing is zeroed everywhere inside the stage, because Godot Labels don't track and any tracking here would quietly inflate every width. Glyph advances are now the engine's, not a browser monospace's.
  • The 10×10 icon art is the real art, pulled from art/sprites/ui/ — elemental types, damage types, injuries. So every icon slot budgets exactly 10px, and anything the game doesn't have can't be drawn here either. Which surfaced a bug in my own data: I'd invented Metal / Wind / Light. The real ramp is Simple · Air · Chivalric · Cold · Electric · Fire · Gentry · Gravity · Heraldic · Occult · Plant · Robo · Void, and the moves are re-typed to it.
  • The measurement caught a real bug. Running every roster and recruit-pool name through the actual font metrics: the card's name slot is rail_width − 59, so a 112px rail gives it 53px — and "Gravity Captain" is 69px, "Healer Goblin" 61px. Both would have clipped. Rail widened to 132px (name slot 73px, longest name 69px), workbench down to 282. That is exactly the kind of thing a monospace stand-in hides until it's built.
  • The rest has room. The compact stat row is 77px against 97px per column, so two columns of four fit with slack. The sheet stacks to ~193px of 336px vertical — ~143px of headroom for the second passive slot, all four injury chips, and longer names. The move slots at two columns give 78px of text room against a 65px worst case ("Compressed Air").
  • Still not measured: borders and paddings here are CSS, not Godot StyleBox insets, so column widths carry ±2px. And the filter row's 16 chips need 276px against 268 available — it wraps to two rows, which is fine but is a wrap, not a fit.
  • Not real either: special/support damage-type icons exist (special_a…special_g, support) but only special_a is used here — that variant set is a decision nobody has made yet.

Workbench placement — the decision

Reading beats swapping (round 12)
  • Your catch was right: the description had secondary billing to a swap the player may not have wanted. Fixed by inverting the workbench — detail on top, bank underneath. Clicking an equipped move is now a pure inspect: the thing you touched gets the headline, full size, with its own icons and copy. Nothing suggests you were about to change anything.
  • Swapping is a second, explicit step. Pick a candidate from the bank and the detail switches to it — because now you're reading about the incoming move — while the swap bar keeps the outgoing one's name and numbers in view: replaces Bonk · Pow 6 R1  [Equip]. Click the same bank row again to drop the candidate and fall back to reading the equipped move.
  • Same inversion helps everywhere: a stat's panel now leads with what the stat does, and the +/− moved onto the sheet row, so allocating never requires the workbench at all. The workbench became the place things are explained, and only sometimes the place they're changed.
  • Filters actually filter — they're the real icons, toggle a few and watch the bank shrink.
  • "LV UP" gone from the XP row — it's just the price now. You're right that a filling bar doesn't need a caption; the gold matches the pool in the top bar, which is the tell that it's a bEXP price and not an XP count.
The PCB trace (your idea, and it earns its keep)
  • Under controller/keyboard aim, a board trace runs from the slot you selected to the panel it opened — orthogonal runs joined by a 45° dogleg, a via pad at each end, 1px azure. Click any slot to see it; switch to the bottom-drawer layout and it re-routes downward.
  • Cursor model only, same doctrine as the main menu: a mouse user just clicked the thing, and a wire chasing the pointer is noise. Press an arrow key to arm it, move the mouse to disarm.
  • I extended it past moves to every slot kind, because the wire turns out to be the clearest possible statement of this screen's one rule — the workbench is downstream of the slot. It's the tutorial, drawn.
  • Motion-gated: the slow travelling dash goes with reduced-motion; the static wire stays.

StatUp tally — pick one

State

The trace also follows the real rule: press an arrow key to arm the cursor model, move the mouse to disarm it.

The one idea
  • Click a slot; the workbench offers what fits in it. No mode toggle, no tabs, no "Editing: Moves | Passives" switch — the lane is implied by what you clicked. Moves, passives, stat-ups and bEXP all land in the same interaction without inventing a third mode. This is what "all the functions in one screen" costs: one column that changes content, two that never do.
  • It supersedes the equipment picker's edit-mode toggle. That was modes-and-verbs — set the mode, then act. This is noun-first: touch the thing you want to change.
  • Stat rows finally get an explainer. Nothing in the game currently tells you what ATH does (it's attack count, not movement). The allocator gives that copy a permanent home at the exact moment the player cares. The tabbed picker had no room for it.
Your two "not sure where to put it" items — both are the rail
  • The sort key is the readout. A 132px rail can't show eight stats, and doesn't need to: sorting by SKL puts SKL on every card, right-aligned, in order. One number per card, always the one you asked for — scanning and sorting become the same act. Cycle it and watch.
  • Default sort is SQUAD ORDER (round 12, your catch). The readout becomes 1, 2, 3… — the order these people joined. It's the only sort key that carries narrative, so the roster doubles as a record of what happened before the campaign started, and the first thing a returning player sees is their crew in the order they met them. Every other key is a tool; this one is also a story. Level-descending is now just another option, and the bEXP deep link overrides to level-ascending on purpose.
  • The stat row is HP  [−]  24 ··  [+] (your spec). The tally is how many StatUps you've already poured in — it has to exist because [−] refunds, and without it there's no telling refundable points from growth rolls. Nothing stretches now: the lightyear was a flex:1 on the tally slot flinging [+] at the far wall. Fixed width, so [+] sits where the number ends. Row lands at 77px.
  • And the left-side awkwardness solves itself. At 77px the row fits two columns of four, so the leftover space goes to the other four stats instead of becoming a gutter — no need for balancing negative space. Split matches StatFingerprint's existing convention (HP/STR/SPC/SKL left, AGL/ATH/DEF/RES right) and it buys back 44px of vertical. Your "stack the +s so they look like a #" instinct is the default tally style — two rows deep, so ten StatUps cost 13px instead of 40.
  • Search is substring, not fuzzy. Predictable beats clever at roster sizes under ~30 — same reason the move bank stays alphabetized. Revisit if a roster passes 30.
  • Benching is a pip, not a button. The old → Bench text button ate a third of the card. Deployed units always sort above the cap line whatever the key; benched units stay in the same rail, dimmed — never on a separate tab, because your own bEXP notes want players not to permanently forget anyone. At cap, bench→deploy pips go inert.
  • Caught while designing this: a sortable rail can't also be the spawn order. Today deployment order decides where units stand on the map — re-sorting to compare AGL would silently rearrange the battlefield. So spawn position gets decoupled and belongs entirely to Show Map. That's a real behavior change from today's prep screen and it needs a test.
Deliberately not in v1
  • Show Map backdrop + spawn reordering — its own surface; the rail work doesn't block it.
  • Squad type-coverage row — top bar is spoken for; add once the rest settles.
  • Compare table (all units × all stats) — the rail's sort readout covers the common case. Say the word if you miss it.

Stat row — Lawrence 2026-08-05, "workshop this"

The second number costs width in a row that measured 77px of 97. Base is capped at 2 digits + the + mark; a 3-digit HP would need a re-measure.

Button set — press both, pick one

What changed under both of these
  • The price tag is gone. Both screens now run the flat pool: 100 bEXP = one level, for anyone. The old 100 × level ÷ squad_max price (which this mockup used to render on the XP row) is retired — it re-introduced "higher level costs more" in bEXP currency, which is the thing you'd argued against. Catch-up lives in XP gain now.
  • Pouring is refundable. Each unit keeps a session baseline plus a poured total, and level/XP are derived from it — so [−] always walks you back, even across a level boundary. Same shape as the StatUp [−] already on the stat rows, which is the point: one interaction idiom for both resources.
  • The pool is shared and live across both tabs. Pour in 2c, switch to 2b, and the top-bar pool and the XP rows already agree. That's not a mockup trick — it's the same state object, which is what the engine would do too.
What I'd watch when you press them
  • 2c shows the whole squad at once; 2b shows one unit at a time. That's the real difference, and it's the thing bEXP might actually need — allocation is a comparison task ("who needs it most?"), and 2b makes you click through units to compare. 2b's bank column tries to close that gap with the squad list, but it's a readout, not a control.
  • Against that: 2c is a screen that does one thing, and it's the second screen in a row the player walks through after a mission. If the hub already deep-links into 2b, 2c is an extra stop that exists only because the layout was convenient.
  • Watch whether you ever want to see a unit's moves while deciding. If yes, 2b wins outright — it has the sheet right there. If bEXP decisions are made purely on level and role, 2c's density wins.
